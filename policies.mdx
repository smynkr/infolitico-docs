Infolitico keeps its reader-facing policies in the product itself. Use this
page as a map to the full policy pages; the linked pages are the authoritative
versions, and the policy pages that carry revision dates show them in the page
header.

## AI disclosure

[AI Disclosure](https://infolitico.com/ai-disclosure) explains how the
automated newsroom works: automated systems discover public news events, pair
them with human-curated Scripture, draft and edit reflections, select or
illustrate artwork, and publish the daily edition. The page frames the
workflow around hard constraints — no story can publish without a verified
source-event record, language models never generate or edit Bible verse text,
and automated guardrails reject drafts that breach the publication's editorial
standards. It also walks through the pipeline's steps, including structured
drafting that separates *The News* from *The Reflection* and *Today's Prayer*,
and visual provenance disclosed in each article's *Behind This Story* panel.
That policy wording is not a complete technical enforcement boundary:
implemented checks cover recognized patterns and do not prove every source or
Scripture claim is error-free. The page also explains the human removal path
and its limits: automated checks reduce risk, but do not make publication
infallible.

## Corrections and retractions

[Retractions and Unpublishing Requests](https://infolitico.com/retractions) is
the contact path for a factual error, misattributed quote, misrepresentation,
privacy concern, or another removal request. Send the story URL to
[retractions@infolitico.com](mailto:retractions@infolitico.com). The product page
asks for a short reason, says that requests receive a response within 24 hours,
and says that the story is unpublished on request.

A story route can show a **Correction notice** when a correction is recorded. A
retracted story URL instead shows a **Retraction notice** explaining that the
story is no longer available. Both notices link back to the corrections policy,
and the public [Corrections ledger](https://infolitico.com/corrections) records
published correction notices. The [Editorial Standards](/infolitico/editorial-standards) page states the
underlying commitment: correct promptly, and unpublish on request when a story
falls short of the standards.

## Community standards

[Community Standards](https://infolitico.com/community-standards) applies to the
separate Infolitico-hosted Reader Feed discussion. Reader comments do not write
to Reddit, and reader email addresses are never displayed publicly — each
participant appears under a consistent pseudonym so conversation stays focused
on ideas rather than clout. The page asks for a charitable-table posture:
grace, honesty, and self-restraint in public conversation. Participants should
address ideas rather than identity or presumed motives and must not post
harassment, threats, personal information, impersonation, repeated promotions,
or deceptive links. A comment may contain no more than two links and should not
be repeated across threads.

Reports request human review; they do not automatically hide a comment.
Operators may hide or restore a comment only through a confirmed workflow that
records an immutable audit entry. Automated rate, duplicate, and link checks
reject a post openly rather than silently publishing and concealing it. Send
questions or appeals to [hello@infolitico.com](mailto:hello@infolitico.com).

## Privacy

[Privacy Policy](https://infolitico.com/privacy) describes cookies and browser
storage, consent-gated analytics and diagnostics, server and security logs,
Feed sign-in and discussion records, newsletter consent, third-party services,
and reader choices. The policy says that passwordless email sign-in is used only
for the separate Reader Feed discussion; Infolitico does not sell reader data,
display sign-in email addresses, or send local comments to Reddit. It also
explains the pseudonym shown on public comments and the contact paths for
account or privacy requests.

## Terms of service

[Terms of Service](https://infolitico.com/terms) govern use of the site, Reader
Feed discussion, morning newsletter, and related content policies. The terms
explain that Infolitico stories pair a real, source-anchored event with a
spiritual reflection and that the reflection is editorial perspective, not a
statement of fact. They also cover professional-advice limits, intellectual
property, third-party links, reader comments, newsletter double opt-in, optional
reader support, disclaimers, and contact addresses.

## About Infolitico

[About Infolitico](https://infolitico.com/about) states the mission: report
today's news accurately and concisely, then connect each story to timeless
wisdom from Scripture and a brief closing prayer. It describes the product as a
faith-framed news publication, names accuracy, faith, Scripture, and
values-based coverage as core beliefs, and distinguishes Infolitico from a
political commentary site, a prosperity-gospel platform, a theology debate
forum, and an outrage machine or end-times speculation blog. A Contact &
Accountability section separates corrections and 24-hour removal requests
([retractions@infolitico.com](mailto:retractions@infolitico.com)) from reader
notes, press inquiries, and general questions
([hello@infolitico.com](mailto:hello@infolitico.com)).

## Shared edition

[How the Edition Works](https://infolitico.com/personalization) explains the
edition's non-personalized model. Infolitico publishes one shared edition for
every reader rather than changing story selection or ordering based on arrival
source, a political label, or a personal profile. Editorial signals — the
event's natural spiritual theme among the twenty theme hubs, its section desk,
how recently a verse or visual motif appeared, and the freshness of the
underlying reporting — keep the edition balanced without profiling readers. It
also documents on-device reader tools that stay local: Reading List bookmarks,
prayers marked "Amen", and the chosen article text size are stored strictly in
browser local storage, never uploaded to a personal recommendation profile. The
page says the site does not build a cross-web identity profile or use hidden
political preferences to decide what a reader sees.
